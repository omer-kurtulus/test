Reconfiguring Cultural Identity in Contemporary Composition

How contemporary composers engage cross-cultural elements to reconfigure cultural identity and instrumental relationships, through interculturality, memory and post-folklore.

Reconfiguring Cultural Identity in Contemporary Composition  Contemporary classical and electroacoustic composers are increasingly engaging with cross-cultural elements to reconfigure cultural identity and instrumental relationships in their music. This research explores how composers reflect or reconstruct historical and cultural selfhood through sound, employing frameworks such as interculturality, inter-instrumentality, memory, post-folklorism, authenticity, and sound-texture conceptualization. Drawing on theories including postcolonialism, phenomenology, and new materialism, this study provides a comprehensive foundation for creative composition grounded in cultural consciousness and sonic innovation.  (Full research content has been truncated for brevity.
